# Solution 1 — Recognizing Curves from Canonical Form

## Problem Statement

Recognize the four curves

$$
x^2+y^2=9,
\qquad
\frac{x^2}{16}+\frac{y^2}{9}=1,
\qquad
y^2=8x,
\qquad
\frac{x^2}{9}-\frac{y^2}{4}=1.
$$

For each curve, identify its main features and describe a sketch.

## Objective

Identify a conic from its standard equation and read off its position, scale, and orientation.

## Concepts and Formulas

A circle centered at the origin has equation $x^2+y^2=r^2$. An ellipse centered at the origin has equation $x^2/a^2+y^2/b^2=1$. A right-opening parabola has equation $y^2=4px$. A horizontal hyperbola has equation $x^2/a^2-y^2/b^2=1$ and asymptotes $y=\pm(b/a)x$.

## Step-by-Step Solution

### 1. Circle

The equation $x^2+y^2=9$ is a circle centered at $(0,0)$ with radius $3$. Its intercepts are $(\pm3,0)$ and $(0,\pm3)$. Sketch a circle of radius $3$ centered at the origin.

### 2. Ellipse

The equation has $a^2=16$ and $b^2=9$, so its semiaxes are $4$ along the $x$-axis and $3$ along the $y$-axis. Its vertices are $(\pm4,0)$ and $(0,\pm3)$. Sketch a horizontally stretched oval centered at the origin.

### 3. Parabola

Compare $y^2=8x$ with $y^2=4px$. Then $p=2$. The vertex is $(0,0)$, the axis is the $x$-axis, and the parabola opens to the right. Its focus is $(2,0)$ and directrix is $x=-2$. Sketch a right-opening curve symmetric about the $x$-axis.

### 4. Hyperbola

Here $a=3$ and $b=2$. The center is $(0,0)$, the vertices are $(\pm3,0)$, and the branches open left and right. Its asymptotes are

$$
y=\pm\frac{2}{3}x.
$$

Sketch two branches opening away from the center and approaching these diagonal lines.

## Verification

The signs and powers identify the types: equal positive squared terms give the circle; unequal positive squared terms give the ellipse; one squared variable equated to a linear variable gives the parabola; and the difference of positive squared terms gives the hyperbola. Substitution of each listed vertex or intercept into its equation confirms the stated scale.

## Final Answer

The equations describe, in order, a circle of radius $3$, a horizontal ellipse with semiaxes $4$ and $3$, a right-opening parabola with $p=2$, and a left-right-opening hyperbola with vertices $(\pm3,0)$ and asymptotes $y=\pm2x/3$.

## Key Takeaways

- The signs of squared terms distinguish the basic conic types.
- Denominators in canonical equations determine semiaxis lengths and asymptote slopes.
- A textual sketch should state the center or vertex, orientation, and key points.

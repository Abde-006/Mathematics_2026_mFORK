# Solution 10 — Paraboloids

## Problem Statement

Compare

$$
z=x^2+y^2,
\qquad
z=x^2-y^2.
$$

For each surface, describe the sections $x=0$, $y=0$, and $z=c$, and give a qualitative sketch.

## Objective

Distinguish a bowl-shaped surface from a saddle by examining vertical and horizontal sections.

## Concepts and Formulas

Fixing a coordinate means substituting its value into the equation. A circle or hyperbola may arise in a horizontal section; a vertical section may be a parabola.

## Step-by-Step Solution

### Surface 1: $z=x^2+y^2$

At $x=0$, the section is $z=y^2$, an upward-opening parabola in the $yz$-plane. At $y=0$, it is $z=x^2$, an upward-opening parabola in the $xz$-plane.

At $z=c$, the equation is $x^2+y^2=c$. If $c>0$, this is a circle of radius $\sqrt c$; if $c=0$, it is the origin; if $c<0$, there are no real points. The sketch is a bowl with vertex at the origin, opening in the positive $z$-direction, with circular horizontal contours widening as $z$ increases.

### Surface 2: $z=x^2-y^2$

At $x=0$, the section is $z=-y^2$, a downward-opening parabola. At $y=0$, the section is $z=x^2$, an upward-opening parabola.

At $z=c$, the section satisfies $x^2-y^2=c$. For $c>0$ it is a hyperbola opening in the $x$-direction; for $c<0$ it opens in the $y$-direction; and for $c=0$ it is the pair of lines $y=x$ and $y=-x$. The sketch is a saddle: it rises along the $x$-axis and falls along the $y$-axis.

## Verification

Substituting $y=0$ or $x=0$ into each surface reproduces the stated parabolas. For the second surface at $c=0$, $x^2-y^2=(x-y)(x+y)=0$, confirming the two lines.

## Final Answer

The first surface is an upward bowl with circular horizontal sections. The second is a saddle with oppositely oriented vertical parabolas and hyperbolic horizontal sections, degenerating to two lines at height zero.

## Key Takeaways

- The signs of the squared terms determine whether vertical sections bend in the same or opposite directions.
- Horizontal sections reveal the circular contours of the bowl and the hyperbolas of the saddle.

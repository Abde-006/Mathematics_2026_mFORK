# Solution 3 - Ellipse

## Problem statement

For

$$
\frac{x^2}{25}+\frac{y^2}{9}=1,
$$

find the semiaxis lengths, the coordinate-axis intercepts, and describe a sketch.

## Lettered quantities

- Let $a>0$ and $b>0$ denote the horizontal and vertical semiaxes.

## Step 1 - Read

**Ellipse form.** In $x^2/a^2+y^2/b^2=1$, $a$ is the horizontal semiaxis and $b$ the vertical semiaxis. Read $a^2$ and $b^2$:
 the denominators

Since $25=5^2$ and $9=3^2$, set

$$
a=5,\qquad b=3.
$$

## Step 2 - Find

**Axis intercept property.** Setting $y=0$ gives $x^2/a^2=1$, hence $x=\pm a$. Apply:
 the $x$-axis intercepts

Set $y=0$ in the equation:

$$
\frac{x^2}{25}=1.
$$

Multiplying by $25$ gives

$$
x^2=25,
\qquad x=\pm5.
$$

The intercepts are $(5,0)$ and $(-5,0)$.

## Step 3 - Find

**Axis intercept reminder.** Setting $x=0$ gives $y=\pm b$. Apply:
 the $y$-axis intercepts

Set $x=0$:

$$
\frac{y^2}{9}=1.
$$

Multiplying by $9$ gives

$$
y^2=9,
\qquad y=\pm3.
$$

The intercepts are $(0,3)$ and $(0,-3)$.

## Verification

Substituting $(5,0)$ gives $25/25+0/9=1$. Substituting $(0,3)$ gives $0/25+9/9=1$. The corresponding negative-axis points give the same values.

## Geometric interpretation

Because $a=5>b=3$, the ellipse is stretched farther along the $x$-axis. This appears in the larger denominator $25$ under $x^2$.

## Final answer

The ellipse has center $(0,0)$, horizontal semiaxis $5$, vertical semiaxis $3$, and intercepts $(\pm5,0)$ and $(0,\pm3)$. Its major axis is the $x$-axis.

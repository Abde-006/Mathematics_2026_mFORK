# Solution 3 — Ellipse

## Problem Statement

For

$$
\frac{x^2}{25}+\frac{y^2}{9}=1,
$$

find the semiaxis lengths and coordinate-axis intercepts, and describe a sketch.

## Objective

Read the size and orientation of the ellipse directly from canonical form.

## Concepts and Formulas

For $x^2/a^2+y^2/b^2=1$, the semiaxis lengths along the $x$- and $y$-axes are $a$ and $b$. Axis intercepts are found by setting the other coordinate to zero.

## Step-by-Step Solution

The denominators are $25=5^2$ and $9=3^2$, so the horizontal semiaxis is $5$ and the vertical semiaxis is $3$.

On the $x$-axis, set $y=0$:

$$
\frac{x^2}{25}=1,
\qquad
x=\pm5.
$$

The intercepts are $(5,0)$ and $(-5,0)$. On the $y$-axis, set $x=0$:

$$
\frac{y^2}{9}=1,
\qquad
y=\pm3.
$$

The intercepts are $(0,3)$ and $(0,-3)$. The ellipse is more stretched along the $x$-axis because $5>3$. Sketch a centered oval with horizontal endpoints at $\pm5$ and vertical endpoints at $\pm3$.

## Verification

Substituting $(\pm5,0)$ gives $25/25=1$; substituting $(0,\pm3)$ gives $9/9=1$. Thus all four endpoints lie on the curve.

## Final Answer

The semiaxis lengths are $5$ horizontally and $3$ vertically. The intercepts are $(\pm5,0)$ and $(0,\pm3)$, and the major axis is the $x$-axis.

## Key Takeaways

- The larger denominator gives the longer semiaxis.
- Setting one coordinate to zero gives the intercepts on the other axis.

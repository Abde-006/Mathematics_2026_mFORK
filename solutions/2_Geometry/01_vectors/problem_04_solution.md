## Exercise 4 — Dot Product and Perpendicularity

### Problem Statement
For $u=(1,2,-1)$ and $v=(2,-1,3)$, compute $u\cdot v$ and decide whether the vectors are perpendicular. Find $a$ so that $w=(2,-1,a)$ is perpendicular to $u$, and check the result.

### Objective
Use the dot product both to test perpendicularity and to determine an unknown coordinate.

### Concepts and Formulas
- The dot product is $x\cdot y=x_1y_1+x_2y_2+x_3y_3$.
- Nonzero vectors are perpendicular exactly when their dot product is zero.

### Step-by-Step Solution

#### Step 1 — Test the given pair

$$
u\cdot v=1(2)+2(-1)+(-1)(3)=2-2-3=-3
$$

Since the dot product is not zero, $u$ and $v$ are not perpendicular.

#### Step 2 — Solve for $a$

For $w=(2,-1,a)$, impose $u\cdot w=0$:

$$
u\cdot w=1(2)+2(-1)+(-1)a=2-2-a=-a
$$

Thus $-a=0$, so $a=0$.

#### Step 3 — Check the resulting vector

$$
u\cdot(2,-1,0)=2-2+0=0
$$

### Verification
The dot product with the specified vector is zero, confirming the required perpendicularity.

### Final Answer
$u\cdot v=-3$, so the given vectors are not perpendicular. The required value is $a=0$.

### Key Takeaways
- A zero dot product is the algebraic test for perpendicular vectors.
- Setting the dot product equal to zero turns perpendicularity into an equation for unknown coordinates.

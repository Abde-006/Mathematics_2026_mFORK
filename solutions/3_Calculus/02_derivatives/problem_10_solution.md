# Problem 10. The Tangent Line as an Application of the Derivative

## Problem statement

For

$$
f(x)=x^2-3x+1,
$$

find the tangent line at the point with $x=2$.

## Named quantities

- $x_0=2$ is the tangency coordinate.
- $y_0=f(x_0)$ is the tangency height.
- $m=f'(x_0)$ is the tangent slope.

## Step 1 - Find the point of tangency

$$
y_0=f(2)=2^2-3(2)+1=4-6+1=-1.
$$

Thus the point is $(2,-1)$.

## Step 2 - Find the derivative and slope

$$
f'(x)=2x-3.
$$

At $x=2$,

$$
m=f'(2)=4-3=1.
$$

## Step 3 - Write the tangent line

**Tangent-line formula.** Given a tangency point $(x_0,y_0)$ and slope $m=f'(x_0)$, the line is $y-y_0=m(x-x_0)$; it is the unique affine line with the graph's instantaneous slope.

$$
y-(-1)=1(x-2).
$$

Simplifying,

$$
y=x-3.
$$

## Verification

Substitute $(2,-1)$ into $y=x-3$:

$$
-1=2-3=-1.
$$

The point lies on the tangent, and the line slope $1$ equals $f'(2)$.

## Interpretation

The tangent rises one unit vertically for each unit of horizontal change at $(2,-1)$.

## Final answer

$$
(2,-1),\qquad f'(2)=1,\qquad y=x-3.
$$

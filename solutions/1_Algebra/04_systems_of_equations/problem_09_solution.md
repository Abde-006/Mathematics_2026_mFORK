# Exercise 9 — A System from a Sum Condition

## Problem Statement

Find $x,y$ satisfying

$$
x+y=10,\qquad 2x+5y=32.
$$

Explain geometrically why the solution is unique.

## Objective

Solve two linear conditions and interpret their solution as the intersection of two lines.

## Concepts and Formulas

Elimination combines equations to remove a variable. Two nonparallel lines in the plane intersect in exactly one point.

## Step-by-Step Solution

### Step 1 — Eliminate $x$

Double the first equation:

$$
2x+2y=20.
$$

Subtract this from the second equation:

$$
(2x+5y)-(2x+2y)=32-20
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad 3y=12,
$$

so $y=4$.

### Step 2 — Find $x$

Substitute $y=4$ into $x+y=10$:

$$
x+4=10\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x=6.
$$

### Step 3 — Explain uniqueness geometrically

The equations describe lines with slopes $-1$ and $-2/5$, respectively. Their slopes differ, so the lines are not parallel and intersect at exactly one point.

## Verification

$$
6+4=10,\qquad 2(6)+5(4)=12+20=32.
$$

## Final Answer

$$
(x,y)=(6,4).
$$

The solution is unique because the two lines have different slopes.

## Key Takeaways

- Elimination reveals the values satisfying both conditions.
- Different slopes guarantee one intersection point for two lines.
